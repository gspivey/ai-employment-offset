# T6: Monte Carlo Uncertainty Quantification -- AI Offset vs Concentration

Generated by `scripts/monte_carlo_t6.py` (seed 2026). Every number below is computed from `data/monte_carlo_raw.csv`; rerun with `--analyze-only` to regenerate this file without re-simulating.

## Design

- **Replications:** N = 2,000 parameter draws, each an AI run and a no-AI counterfactual from the same burned-in economy and seed (common random numbers). 60 extra replications at fixed default parameters measure pure simulation noise.
- **Outcomes:** AI-vs-counterfactual proportional differences averaged over the last 10 of 40 periods (AI starts at period 5; diffusion S > 0.99 throughout the window).
- **Priors (independent):** sigma_ai ~ U(2, 5); entry_cost_post = entry_cost_pre x U(0.7, 1.0) (entry_cost_pre = 20, so a 0-30% cut); ai_entrant_productivity_shift ~ U(0, 0.15); ai_adoption_fixed_cost ~ U(2, 10).
- **Held fixed at `Parameters()` defaults:** alpha = 0.64 (displacement threshold 1/(1-alpha) = 2.778), ai_cost_full = 1.5, entry_pool_elasticity = 0.3, sector exposures (0.5, 0.8, 1.2, 1.5). Labour supply is perfectly elastic (partial equilibrium), so `wage_effect` is 0 by construction.
- **Definitions:** offset = net_employment_effect > 0; strong offset = net > 5%; concentration = employment in firms with < 20 employees falls (small_firm_employment_effect < 0). Size classes use *current* size buckets (small < 20, mid 20-499, large 500+), not pre-shock cohorts.

## Key findings

1. **P(offset) = 81.7%** (95% CI 79.9%-83.3%). Median net employment effect +10.59%, mean +14.21%, 90% interval [-5.83%, +51.65%]. Smoothing out simulation noise with a quartic response surface (R^2 = 0.93, residual SD 4.7% vs pure-noise SD 4.2%) gives P(offset) = 83.2%, so the headline is not a noise artefact.
2. **P(strong offset, net > 5%) = 66.9%** (95% CI 64.8%-68.9%).
3. **Most offset is absence of displacement, not compensation for it.** Large-firm (500+) employment falls in only 43.5% of draws. Regimes: no large-firm displacement 56.5%; displacement fully offset 25.8%; displacement not offset 17.8%. Conditional on large-firm employment falling, P(offset) = 59.2% (95% CI 55.9%-62.5%); median offset-ratio proxy among those draws = 1.47.
4. **P(concentration) = 4.0%** (95% CI 3.2%-4.9%), and it never coincides with job loss: P(no offset and concentration) = 0.0%. It occurs at low sigma_ai, where AI is a large cost advantage and adopters scale up (sigma_ai <= 2.5: adoption 43.4%, 500+ employment +70.12%, P(concentration) 22.1%). This is concentration through growth: firms climb out of the <20 bucket. Share-based concentration (500+ share of employment rises) is more common, 38.0% overall, 80.4% for sigma_ai < 2.78 vs 22.1% above. Small-firm (<20) employment rises in 96.0% of draws (median +12.27%).
5. **sigma_ai (labour-AI substitution) dominates the net effect.** First-order index S1 = 71% (Spearman rho = -0.66). Next is AI entrant productivity shift (S1 = 18%), then entry-cost reduction (1 - post/pre) (S1 = 3%) and AI adoption fixed cost (S1 = <1%). The four parameters explain 65% of variance linearly and 86% with a quadratic surface; pure simulation noise is 5.8% of the total. For small-firm employment the entry-side channels matter about as much as sigma_ai: sigma_ai (labour-AI substitution) S1 = 31%, AI entrant productivity shift S1 = 30%, entry-cost reduction (1 - post/pre) S1 = 25%, AI adoption fixed cost S1 = 6%.
6. **No sigma_ai tipping point inside the prior.** The binned mean net effect never crosses zero on [2, 5]: it falls from +55.38% at sigma_ai in [2.00, 2.25] to +4.08%-+7.04% for sigma_ai >= 3.25, and P(offset) never falls below 65.9%. Higher sigma_ai displaces more labour per adopter but also shrinks AI's cost advantage, so adoption falls from 49.8% to 9.6% across the range (with ai_cost_full = 1.5 the AI price is at or above the wage in every sector). Below the single-firm threshold 1/(1-alpha) = 2.78: P(offset) = 99.8% (n = 547); above it: 74.9% (n = 1453).
7. **Offset fails mainly when high substitution meets weak AI-native entry.** sigma_ai > 3.5 and entrant shift < 0.05 (n = 316): P(offset) = 27.8%, mean net -2.77%. Entrant shift >= 0.10 (n = 689): P(offset) = 99.3%. Above the threshold, entrant quality decides offset; the size of the entry-cost cut matters far less (S1 = 3%).

### Caveats

- **Simulation noise is material where the effect is small.** For sigma_ai >= 3.25 the net effect has SD 8.5%, of which pure noise (SD 4.2%) is 25% of the variance. The model economy has about 175k workers, and the 500+ bucket (25% of employment) holds few firms, so shock histories move it. P(offset) in that region mixes parameter uncertainty with finite-economy luck.
- **Partial equilibrium.** Wages are fixed at wage0. A finite labour-supply elasticity would damp both gains and losses.
- **ai_cost_full is fixed at 1.5.** It sets how cheap AI is relative to labour and drives the adoption decline that flattens the sigma_ai profile. `default_uncertainty()` already carries a lognormal prior for it, and it is the obvious next parameter to add.
- **Size classes are current-size buckets, not pre-shock cohorts.** `offset_ratio_proxy` is (change in <500 employment) / (loss in 500+ employment), so it only approximates the paper's cohort-based Omega. Firms that grow across a bucket edge count as gains in one class and losses in another.
