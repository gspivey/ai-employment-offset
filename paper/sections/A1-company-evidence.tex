\paragraph{Infrastructure layer.}
Anthropic's S-1 reports compute and infrastructure costs equal to 48\% of revenue in the second quarter of 2026 \citep{anthropic2026s1}, and CoreWeave carried about \$35.1 billion of debt at June 2026, with Microsoft accounting for 67\% of its 2025 revenue \citep{coreweave2026tenk,coreweave2026tenq}.
Much of the employment this spending supports is at chip makers, hyperscalers, and data-center builders, which are old, large firms.
Microsoft offered voluntary exits to about 60,000 employees in October 2026, an eligibility figure rather than an exit count, while guiding calendar-2026 capital expenditure to about \$175 billion \citep{techcrunch2026msftbuyout,msft2026fy26q4}; these aggregates are consistent with trading headcount for capital but cannot establish it.
Anthropic's own headcount rose from about 940 (December 2024) to about 5,300 (September 2026) as it reported that most of its code was written by its models \citep{anthropic2026s1}; a single fast-growing firm cannot identify $\sigma_{\text{ai}}$.

\paragraph{Exits.}
Several fast-growing AI firms have been absorbed by large incumbents before reaching scale as independent employers: SpaceX completed its acquisition of Cursor in August 2026 \citep{spacex2026cursoragreement,spacex2026cursorclose}, Inflection's co-founders and team joined Microsoft in 2024 \citep{microsoft2024inflection}, and Google licensed Character.ai's technology and hired its co-founders \citep{mehta2024characterai}.
In BDS terms, the acquired employment becomes employment at old, large firms, which bears on how the offset ratio would be measured by firm size and age.
