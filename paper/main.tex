\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Can firm formation induced by artificial intelligence (AI) offset the labor that large incumbents shed as they adopt it?
We study a partial-equilibrium firm-dynamics model in which AI is both a labor-saving input at adopting firms and a cut in the cost of entry.
Wages are fixed, so every outcome is a change in labor demand, not in equilibrium employment, and the model is not calibrated.
Whether an adopter hires or sheds workers is the Hicks--Marshall comparison of the labor--AI substitution elasticity with the firm's scale elasticity; with span-of-control returns $\alpha = 0.64$ the threshold is $2.78$, but it rises steeply with $\alpha$.
Holding the cost saving from AI fixed as the elasticity varies, the net labor-demand effect declines monotonically in the elasticity: offset fails when substitution is high and AI-native entrants are no more productive than earlier entrants, and cheaper entry matters little.
In Census business applications, two highly AI-exposed sectors pulled away from other sectors from March 2025, by $0.29$ log points in 2026, mostly among applicants unlikely to hire; the estimate is sensitive to which sectors count as exposed.
\end{abstract}
